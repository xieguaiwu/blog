You come to realize, with increasing clarity, that this melancholy is not a foreign body that can be eradicated. It is not a disease in any substantive sense, though it does involve organic changes... Between spells of delirium and icy fervor, you understand that it has always here. You once lived without it; now it will be with you forever. And so you can only endure in silence the absurd effects it produces — from the estranged gaze of self-examination (indeed, most social anxiety derives from precisely this self-examination) to the stupid lies of pseudoscience — you are compelled to treat it as a metaphysical alien presence, unable to fully acknowledge that it is part of yourself... No matter how many times the chain of reasoning brings you to this conclusion, you still hold it at arm's length in the drift of daily life, because there is no alternative: you have to get better. You must get better.

A

"The gaze is like
a crack — through this crack, gas flows unhindered, and a paralyzing, piercingly cold poisonous wind blows in and fills the entire space. In the tense silence, one hears only the incessant buzzing of flies and the ceaseless, torrential rain pouring down from above. All the small, frequently recurring sounds combine these two: outside, in the twisted locust trees, between all the table legs and counter brackets, a special nocturnal labor is underway. They measure the smallest units of time with irregular rhythms and mercilessly distribute these spaces, so that each word, each sentence, or each action finds its proper place. This night at the end of October admits only one rhythm: an order beyond the reach of any word or imagination."

It has been a full year since the formal diagnosis, through withdrawal from school and return. The present moment is a low point after three consecutive months devoted to coursework, but rather than a deterioration of my condition, it would be more accurate to say that the closed space I had accepted as normal for over a decade — and the rules governing that closed space — have devastated the private order I had so painstakingly built. Interestingly, in the process of investigating the ideological mechanisms at work, I developed an aesthetic experience of dehumanization: though fully aware that this mechanism has devastated many people, including myself, I nonetheless marvel at the exquisitely wrought structure that an unconscious collective has produced through sheer passive drift: on one side, the value system of impatient utility and the individual's unconscious resistance to it (deliberate omissions within explicit rules and the reign of unspoken codes); on the other, the fully enclosed collective living arrangements adopted at the material level to save on the costs of management. This peculiar aesthetic had in fact manifested long before... During the months of withdrawal from school, apart from the initial weeks of psychic paralysis, alongside vast reading and unmoored thinking, I spent enormous amounts of time wandering through Wikipedia pages, hunting for the special moments crystallized in photographs from twentieth-century history (particularly the reign of fascism and the GPCR and similar episodes of violence) — "For the collector, nothing is more enchanting than sealing a solitary object within a magic circle, where the object remains immobile, and the keenest excitement, the thrill of acquisition, passes over it. Everything thought or remembered, every act of understanding, becomes the pedestal, the frame, the foundation, and the lock of his property" / "The image of the past that is driven by the wind can only be captured as an image that flashes in the moment of recognizability and then vanishes forever."

Nevertheless, I know this is not a chaotic era like that of a century ago. In fact, for those of us living in this particular region and struggling to integrate into the forcibly delineated mainstream, the greatest problem lies precisely in the fact that its order is, on the one hand, so stable, and on the other, has crushed us into our present state precisely through the oppression of that stable order. Even if the next world war never comes, the description in *All Quiet on the Western Front* remains unchanged:

"And it is this silence that awakens in memory not longing, but sorrow — a vast, inexpressible despondency. We longed for it once, but we shall no longer long for it. It has become the past, another world, a world that has vanished for us. On the drill ground, the memory of days gone by once aroused a rebellious, wild longing. Then we were still connected to it. Though we had already taken our leave, we belonged to them, and they belonged to us. They appeared in the military songs we sang as we marched in step through the morning glow and black shadows of trees to the wilderness for drill — deep, heartfelt yearnings buried within."

I often feel like a survivor of a flesh-and-blood war of memory — my will, from this side, remembering a past it can never reach, while also suspecting the beauty held in memory. But just as the very foundation on which this beauty was established is itself suspect, so too this suspicion is a fruit of retrospection... Having clarified all of this, I have finally understood my situation: this is a desperate, irrevocable journey down a river that flows in one direction, beginning in dim speech and ending in the language of the dying.

B

"The feeling of expansion toward nothingness that arises in states of melancholy has its roots in the exhaustion characteristic of all negative states. This exhaustion separates the human being from the world. The vigorous rhythm of life, its organic inner pulse, is weakened."

I have come to realize, with ever-greater clarity, how peculiar my state of selfhood is... From what precedes, one can readily grasp the general outline of the first state of selfhood: an inhuman detachment, a depletion of vitality centered on the aesthetic, and incessant self-articulation. Within this consciousness that nearly bursts through the body, all thinking is shallow, for it consists merely of glaciated ravines yoked together by a series of vague sensations, shaped by the collage of copious quotations and received materials — leaving behind only a translucent shadow of existence, without form, yet perhaps harboring life. Having become aware of this, what follows is a second state of selfhood, originating in introspection, advancing through thought and deriving pleasure from it — the state most genuinely suited to real thinking. Over the months of recovery, I relied on it to pursue my interests, yet also, precisely because of it, constantly questioned the authenticity of that pleasure: What are the consequences of stopping? What is the meaning of stopping? Though I knew with absolute certainty that it could restore me to vitality, when the last day of such living arrived, I still needed to tear open the wound that had only just begun to heal. The resolution of this interrogation is found in the further advancement of the second state... Unlike the first state, the second is not necessarily submerged in melancholy; at times it even manifests as fervor, and in that fervor it burns itself out completely, advancing into a third state.

C

"The feverish activity and anxious restlessness of work destroy our inner sense of eternity. Work is the negation of eternity. The more material goods we acquire in the secular realm, the more intense our external labor, the more inaccessible and remote eternity becomes."

From July to November, I channeled the tenacity and concentration of the second state into the repetitive labor of preparing for the re-entry examination. During this period, reading was dominated by the Goebbels biography, mostly historical scholarship, but the sole purpose of reading was to obtain the impetus to carry on. I could not be easily deceived by the motivational content found everywhere on the internet, because I knew its producers all too well — no matter how immense the success they claimed, they could not even begin to compare with myself. You might say this was merely a phase of adolescent arrogance, but I would not agree: what exactly do those self-proclaimed "strivers" have to be proud of? Most of what drags them into the trough is nothing more than base physiological desires; before they re-enter the discursive system as victors through all-night repetitive labor, they wallow in unreflective video games, livestreams, or web novels, toiling diligently like hamsters running in place on a wheel for a lick of sugar water — and study and work are much the same to them. They are precisely the typical products molded by this society (though it uses value systems diametrically opposed to its economic base to paint an intensely conservative pastoral picture): a group of "celebrities of consumption, who are simultaneously, on the outside, expressions of different personality types, demonstrating that every type can equally arrive at the totality of consumption and find its happiness there. The celebrity who makes decisions must hold a full inventory, piled with approved human qualities. Thus in human qualities, official differences are abolished by official sameness, which presupposes that human beings are omnipotent"... at times acridly sarcastic, taking petty satisfaction in some slight superiority over others, as if stranded in a primitive jungle, desperately striving to fashion oneself into a successful predator; at times wearing a hypocritical smile, dispensing banal advice — "to open the eyes of the blind, to make the lame walk" — as if guarding the dreadful oasis at the end of a vast wasteland, requiring a process of proselytization and trial before travelers and supplicants may pass through in peace, only to discover abruptly that all of this is merely a beautiful hallucination at the threshold of death; at times pacing restlessly before a mirror, longing to make these glories that never truly belong to oneself linger a little longer, and thus ever more desperately performing the role of oneself.

This is what "fully recovered" looked like over those four months, and now too the third state is on the verge of collapse, panting in the gaps of drift, awaiting the next cycle's beginning.

Nevertheless, I still long for the coming of final redemption — otherwise I would not be typing this last word at this very moment, waiting for the network to carry it to an unpredictable shore. I know that I can climb out of my rubble-strewn psyche time and again; I believe that my will, my discipline, and my intellect will keep me from falling to the very bottom. But I do not know where such a future lies: does every expenditure of total effort inevitably lead to the discovery that it cannot last? Must one, after sinking into this comfortable numbness, always be compelled to continue conforming to rules, knowing full well that this is the source of most suffering? Must one willingly remain trapped in this vortex of a binary opposition between "recovery" and "illness" that has erased one's own specificity?
